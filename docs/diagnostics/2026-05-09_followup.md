**Question**: Does the original D1 PASS verdict hold up given that replay used the `_rule_regime` fallback instead of the live HMM regime detector? Specifically — do any voters branch on regime?

**Method**: Source-code inspection of [trading/live_paper_runner.py:498-619](trading/live_paper_runner.py#L498-L619). No code execution, no replay re-run.

| Voter | Reads Regime? | Branch Type | Where in code | Effect on output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `vote_ema` | **YES** | **Signal direction** | line 510 (`BULL_TREND`), line 512 (`BEAR_TREND`) | In non-cross cases, BUY emitted **only if** `regime == "BULL_TREND"` AND `rsi < 68`. Same for SELL with `BEAR_TREND`. Without correct regime label, voter falls back to HOLD. |

| `vote_vwap` | **YES** | **Signal gating** | line 561, 565 | BUY emitted **only if** regime in (BULL_TREND, RANGE_BOUND); SELL emitted **only if** regime in (BEAR_TREND, HIGH_VOLATILITY). Wrong regime → HOLD regardless of price-vs-VWAP deviation. |
| `vote_bollinger` | **YES** | Confidence weight only | line 581 | `w = 0.78 if RANGE_BOUND else 0.42`. Halves confidence outside range. Signal direction unchanged. |
| `vote_macd_hist` | **YES** | Confidence weight only | line 608, 612 | `w = 0.74 if (BULL_TREND, RANGE_BOUND) else 0.45` for BUY; same for SELL with (BEAR_TREND, HIGH_VOLATILITY). Signal direction unchanged. |

**Helper functions**: None of the 6 voters call other helpers that read regime internally. `_vote()` is a pure packaging function. No global regime cache reads.

### Verdict on D1's PASS

**6 of 6 voters read regime. D1 PASS now has a meaningful asterisk.**

- **Signal-redundancy claim still holds** (PASS): voters look at distinct features — EMA crossover vs RSI level vs return×MACD vs VWAP deviation vs Bollinger %B vs MACD histogram momentum.
- **But the replay used `_rule_regime` fallback** (HMM cache empty for historical bars). Two voters in particular would behave differently in production if the HMM produces materially different labels:
  - `vote_ema` — different regime label can flip a non-cross BUY/SELL to HOLD (or vice versa).
  - `vote_vwap` — different regime can entirely gate out the signal direction.
- The other 4 voters preserve their signal direction across regimes but their **confidence weights** shift, which affects the consensus tally indirectly via `_voter`'s 0.45 agreement threshold.

**What this means**: D1's correlation finding (avg|r|=0.33, max 0.65) is a **lower bound under the rule-fallback regime path**. Under the live HMM path, the correlations could shift in either direction. Without saving HMM regime labels alongside historical bars (or running both detectors side-by-side on replay), we cannot tighten this bound.

## Check 2 — SOL/AVAX Rolling-Window Cointegration Stability

**Question**: D3 found SOL/AVAX cointegrated on a single 90d snapshot (p=0.013, corr=0.91). Is this a stable property or did the snapshot catch a lucky window?

**Method**: 365 days × 4H Binance OHLCV via ccxt. Rolling 30-day windows stepped by 7 days (48 overlapping windows) AND non-overlapping 30-day windows (12 disjoint windows). Per window: Engle-Granger p-value, log-price Pearson correlation, end-of-window spread z-score.

### Headline numbers — SOL/USDT vs AVAX/USDT

| Metric | Overlapping (48 windows, step 7d) | Non-overlapping (12 windows) |
|---|---|---|
| % windows with p<0.05 AND \|corr\|>0.80 | **8.33%** (4 of 48) | **8.33%** (1 of 12) |
| Median p-value | 0.367 | 0.538 |
| Median log-price correlation | 0.904 | 0.892 |
| Longest pass streak (consecutive overlapping windows) | **2 windows** (~2 weeks) | — |
| Longest fail streak (consecutive overlapping windows) | **27 windows** (~6 months) | — |

**Plots**:
- ![SOL/AVAX rolling p-value](d4_sol_avax_pvalue.png)
- ![SOL/AVAX rolling correlation](d4_sol_avax_corr.png)

### Verdict: ❌ **UNSTABLE**

**The 90-day snapshot from D3 caught the pair at the tail end of one of its rare 2-week pass windows.** Across 12 months:
- The pair was **not cointegrated** (p > 0.05) in **>91% of windows**.
- The longest cointegration streak was **2 consecutive overlapping windows** (~2 weeks).
- The longest non-cointegration streak was **27 consecutive overlapping windows** (~6 months).
- Correlation alone is consistently high (median ~0.90) — but cointegration is what stat-arb mathematically requires, not just correlation. This pair co-moves but rarely mean-reverts.

**Plain-English interpretation**: Treating SOL/AVAX as a viable C1 replacement pair would mean entering during a ~2-week window of cointegration and likely getting trapped when the next 6-month decoupling phase starts. The 90-day snapshot D3 produced was a **selection-on-the-dependent-variable artifact** — we asked "show me the best pair on the most recent 90d" and got a pair whose recent 90d happened to overlap with one of its rare cointegrated phases. Forward-looking, the prior probability of any random month being a tradeable month for SOL/AVAX is ~8%.

## Known Limitations

- **12-month window may not capture full crypto regime cycles.** Crypto markets have shown 2-4 year cycles historically; 12 months covers ~1/3 of a typical macro cycle. A pair that is unstable over 12 months might be stable over a different 12-month period (or unstable over a 4-year period). Conclusions are scoped to the last 12 months.
- **Binance-only data.** Liquidity and price discovery for these symbols differ across exchanges. A pair that is cointegrated on Binance might not be on Coinbase or Kraken (different fee structures, market-maker behavior, liquidation cascades). Not tested.
- **Spot prices only — no perpetual basis or funding effects considered.** Stat-arb on spot vs spot ignores that real traders may simultaneously trade perpetuals, which can create temporary dislocations that bias short-window cointegration tests.
- **Engle-Granger is one of several cointegration tests.** Johansen's test (multivariate, cointegration vector estimation) might give different results, especially for pairs with structural breaks. We report only EG.
- **Hedge ratio used in z-score is OLS, not Kalman.** A Kalman-filtered hedge ratio that adapts to drift might find more cointegration. Not tested.
- **No look-ahead correction in the rolling test itself**: each window's p-value uses only data within that window — but the *choice* to test these particular pairs was informed by the D3 90-day result, which is itself a form of selection bias. The follow-up confirms D3's finding was selection-biased; it doesn't free us from selection bias entirely.
- **Window size = 30 days is somewhat arbitrary.** Live `trading.stat_arb` uses a window of 30 bars (not days) for the z-score — different time horizon. Cointegration tests typically need 60+ observations to be reliable; with 4H bars, 30 days = 180 bars, comfortably above that floor. But shorter windows would be even noisier.
- **What's NOT tested by D4**: time-of-day effects (Asia/EU/US session boundaries), structural breaks (forks, listings, delistings), regime-conditional cointegration (stat-arb might be tradeable in BULL but not BEAR), and any of the things the original report flagged (look-ahead, label leakage, walk-forward stability).
